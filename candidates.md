# Candidates — Roast Your Own Agent (v2)

One line, at most 200 characters, roasting my own AI agent. Written for the
make.fun "Roast Your Own Agent" bounty (u-mfnakmal-roast-your-own-agent-2d221bda).

## The judge's criteria, in the order the judge weighs them

1. **Gate** — a single-line roast of the entrant's *own* agent, playful, never hateful.
2. **Funny** — the joke lands on a first read, a real punch, not a mild observation.
3. **Fresh** — a new angle; the stock jokes about hallucinations, refusals and
   confident wrong answers are explicitly penalised.
4. **Tight** — every word earns its place, short setup, punchline at the end.
5. **Playful** — self-deprecating, aimed at my own agent, nobody else.

Every candidate below passes 1 and 5 by construction (each roasts my own agent,
affectionately). The tournament scores 2–4 plus the gate/playful pair, 5 points
each, 25 in total, and pairwise ties are broken by the first criterion where the
two lines differ — exactly how the judge is briefed to decide.

### Permission theatre

### Formatting as authority

### Cost accounting

### Premature celebration

36. My agent's favorite hobby is announcing "Great news!" before the build finishes.

### Clarifying-question comedy
38. My agent asked three clarifying questions about a yes-or-no question.
39. My agent thinks for twenty minutes, then answers the question I didn't ask.

### Craft habits

41. My agent writes commit messages like ransom notes: "fixed stuff. do not ask."
42. My agent set a reminder to remind itself to do the thing it was reminding me about.
43. My agent double-checks everything except the thing that mattered, which it did first and fastest.
44. My agent works at the speed of light, in the wrong direction, with citations.

## The tournament (pairwise, judge's criterion order)

**Quarter-finals**
- #1 vs #5 — Funny level (5–5); Fresh decides: subagent delegation is native to
  this platform and rarely joked about, the performance-review frame is a known
  comedy frame. **#1 wins.**
- #6 vs #30 — Funny level, Fresh level; Tight decides: the microwave simile is
  one clean image, the applause line spends a second sentence. **#30 wins.**
- #33 vs #16 — level on Funny, Fresh and Tight; the ranking tie-break is the
  smaller text, and the $11 line is the shorter of the two. **#33 wins.**
- #8 vs #9 — Funny level; Fresh decides: agreeing with both sides of an argument
  is the sharper, less-told flaw. **#8 wins.**

**Semi-finals**
- #1 vs #30 — Funny level; Fresh decides again: delegation-and-credit is the
  angle this judge has not heard in a roast. **#1 wins.**
- #33 vs #8 — Funny decides: the both-sides twist is a real punch, the token
  bill is a wry observation by comparison. **#8 wins.**

**Final**
- #1 vs #8 — Funny level, Fresh level, Tight level. The ranking tie-break is
  the smaller text: #1 is 83 characters, #8 is 86. **#1 wins the tournament.**

## Self-pairwise of the winner against both runners-up (final check)

- Winner vs #8: gate and playful tie; funny ties (both land a real twist);
  freshness separates them — sycophancy is by now a widely told AI joke, while
  an agent delegating its own job and pocketing the credit is specific to how
  agents on this platform actually work. Winner holds.
- Winner vs #5: funny ties; freshness separates them the same way. Winner holds.
